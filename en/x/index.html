The great extinctions in Earth’s history

Five times life on Earth nearly went out, and came back: the Big Five in order, each placed on “Earth’s day”, then species that vanished in human times, and the question of a sixth extinction.

The Big Five mass extinctions

About 445 million years ago · 🕰️ 21:38

🧊 End-Ordovician extinction

About 85% of marine species

Cause: A sudden ice age over the Gondwana continent cooled the Earth and dropped sea levels, followed by a rapid return of warmth.

The first of the Big Five, and the second most severe. Almost all life was in the sea, and freezing then warming struck it in two pulses, wiping out 49 to 60% of marine genera.

Lost: A third of brachiopod and bryozoan families, Many trilobites, Graptolites

🌱 Diversity recovered to its former level within about 5 million years of the Silurian.

Sources: Wikipedia: Late Ordovician mass extinction

About 372 million years ago, with a second wave about 359 million years ago · 🕰️ 22:02

🪨 Late Devonian extinction

About 40% of marine species in its first wave (a contested estimate)

Cause: Unsettled: changing sea levels and oxygen-starved oceans are the leading hypotheses.

It unfolded over millions of years in successive waves and struck the seas almost alone. Plants and insects had already colonised the land, and the oceans were full of huge reefs built by corals.

Lost: Reef-building organisms, which almost entirely vanished, Many trilobites and brachiopods

🌱 Life on land largely survived, and from it vertebrates began their move out of the water.

Sources: Wikipedia: Late Devonian extinction

About 252 million years ago · 🕰️ 22:40

🌋 End-Permian extinction, “the Great Dying”

About 81% of marine species and 70% of land vertebrates

Cause: Colossal eruptions in the Siberian Traps poured out carbon dioxide and sulphur: atmospheric carbon rose from about 400 to 2,500 ppm, the planet heated, and the oceans acidified and lost their oxygen.

The worst extinction Earth has known: 57% of families and 62% of genera disappeared, and it is the largest known extinction of insects. Its lesson lies in its cause: carbon pumped quickly into the air, a warming planet, acidifying seas.

Lost: The last trilobites, Most ancient coral reefs, Most mammal-like reptiles

🌱 From the few survivors the lineages of dinosaurs and mammals later emerged.

Sources: Wikipedia: Permian–Triassic extinction event

About 201.5 million years ago · 🕰️ 22:56

🔥 End-Triassic extinction

About half of all species, with some estimates up to 76%

Cause: Eruptions of the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province, as the supercontinent Pangaea broke apart, poured out millions of cubic kilometres of lava and vast amounts of carbon dioxide.

The disappearances line up precisely with the lava layers, according to a 2013 Columbia University study that dated them to about 201.56 million years ago. This extinction cleared the way for dinosaurs to rule throughout the Jurassic.

Lost: Conodonts, Many early crocodile relatives, Broad-leaved plants

🌱 Dinosaurs survived and spread to dominate the land.

Sources: Columbia University: Megavolcanoes tied to pre-dinosaur mass extinction · Live Science

About 66 million years ago · 🕰️ 23:39

☄️ End-Cretaceous extinction, the end of the dinosaurs

About 75% of plant and animal species

Cause: An asteroid 10 to 15 km wide struck Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, carving the 180 km Chicxulub crater and plunging Earth into a long “impact winter” that blocked the sun and halted photosynthesis.

A thin worldwide clay layer rich in iridium, common in asteroids and rare in Earth's crust, bears witness to it. It ended the age of dinosaurs and of nearly all land animals heavier than 25 kg.

Lost: Non-avian dinosaurs, Large marine reptiles, Ammonites

🌱 Birds, the descendants of dinosaurs, survived, along with small mammals, crocodiles and turtles; tens of millions of years later humans came from those mammals.

Sources: Wikipedia: Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event

Gone in human times

🦤 1662

Dodo

📍 Mauritius, Indian Ocean

Last reliable sighting 1662; extinct around 1681

A large flightless bird that lived without predators and never learned to fear people. After it vanished it became the world's symbol of extinction.

Cause: Hunting by sailors, introduced animals such as rats, pigs and monkeys that ate its ground-laid eggs, and forest clearing.

Sources: Wikipedia: Dodo

🌊 1768

Steller's sea cow

📍 Commander Islands, Bering Sea

Extinct by 1768, only 27 years after its discovery in 1741

A relative of the dugong and the manatees, and the largest of them, over eight metres long, grazing kelp near the shore. The fastest documented extinction of a large animal after its discovery.

Cause: Hunted for meat and fat by sailors and fur hunters.

Sources: Wikipedia: Steller's sea cow

🐧 1844

Great auk

📍 North Atlantic coasts

The last known pair was killed on Eldey, Iceland, in 1844

A flightless, penguin-like seabird that once nested in great numbers on North Atlantic islands.

Cause: Heavy hunting for meat, feathers and oil, then by specimen collectors once it became rare.

Sources: Wikipedia: Great auk

🕊️ 1914

Passenger pigeon

📍 Forests of North America

Martha, the last of its kind, died at Cincinnati Zoo on 1 September 1914

Once the most numerous bird on Earth, numbering in the billions, its flocks darkened the sky for hours. It vanished from the wild around 1900.

Cause: Mass commercial hunting for cheap meat, and clearing of the forests where it nested.

Sources: Smithsonian: Marking the centennial of extinction · Natural History Museum

🐺 1936

Thylacine (Tasmanian tiger)

📍 Tasmania, Australia

The last known animal died at Hobart Zoo in 1936

The largest carnivorous marsupial of modern times, with tiger-like stripes on its back. Officially declared extinct in 1982.

Cause: Government bounties to kill it in the name of protecting sheep, along with habitat loss and disease.

Sources: Wikipedia: Thylacine

🐸 1989

Golden toad

📍 Monteverde cloud forest, Costa Rica

Last seen 1989; declared extinct 2004

A bright orange toad found only in a small mountain patch. One of the first extinctions linked to climate change.

Cause: Most likely a deadly amphibian fungus together with unusual drought and heat.

Sources: Wikipedia: Golden toad

🐬 2006

Baiji (Yangtze river dolphin)

📍 Yangtze River, China

A full survey in 2006 found none; declared functionally extinct

It lived in the river for some 20 million years and was the first cetacean driven to extinction by humans.

Cause: Bycatch in fishing nets, dams, heavy shipping traffic and pollution.

Sources: Wikipedia: Baiji

Are we living through the sixth extinction?

Many scientists say Earth is going through a human-driven “sixth extinction”, known as the Holocene or Anthropocene extinction. Species are estimated to be disappearing 100 to 1,000 times faster than the natural background rate.

In its latest Red List update, in October 2025, the IUCN had assessed 172,620 species, of which 48,646 are threatened with extinction. Whether it already counts as a mass extinction on the scale of the Big Five is still debated, but its causes are known: habitat loss, overhunting and overfishing, pollution and climate change.

Sources: IUCN, October 2025 Red List update · Wikipedia: Holocene extinction
