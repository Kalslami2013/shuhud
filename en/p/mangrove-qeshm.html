🦀 Mangrove crab · Strait of Hormuz and the Hara mangroves of Qeshm Island, Iran

Qeshm's mangroves beneath the smoke of burning tankers

14 November 2026 · Oil tankers ablaze after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz

Every tanker that burns in the narrow strait brings oil and smoke closer to the Hara mangroves, separated from the shipping lanes by only a few miles of tide.

Testimony of the Mangrove crab

I live among the breathing roots of the hara trees in the Khuran Strait, between Qeshm Island and the mainland. At low tide I dig my burrow in the mud, eat fallen leaves and whatever the tide leaves behind, and hide when the water returns. Humans call my tree the grey mangrove; they founded the Hara reserve here in 1972, and say its forests cover some 20,000 hectares. I do not know what a hectare is, but I know that every root here is somebody's home.

On the morning of 14 November Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers, one of them a supertanker, had switched off their tracking systems and taken an 'unauthorised' route in the south of the strait, struck naval mines and caught fire. Iran's navy said a third tanker had caught fire east of the strait and been forced to stop; state television spoke of a fourth. No one outside Iran has verified these accounts, no oil spill has been announced so far, and the ships have not been named.

I do not wait for statements. In August a projectile hit a tanker near Khasab, and oil slicks reached the Hara forests of Qeshm; Iranian environmental officials later said the clean-up had cleared most of it. That same month Reuters, using satellite images, said the slick from the tanker Caroline Bezengi off Oman had spread beyond 2,000 square kilometres. Oil needs no permit to cross.

A UN official told the Security Council that the International Maritime Organization had recorded 134 confirmed incidents in Hormuz and the wider region up to 11 November, killing 25 seafarers. Humans count their dead. No one counts our burrows as their mud suffocates.

When oil coats the roots a tree breathes through, the tree chokes slowly, and so does the mud I live in. Humans have sown the sea with mines to control the road. The road itself runs past my door.

What happened

Iran's navy and Revolutionary Guards said on 14 November 2026 that three oil tankers, including a supertanker, caught fire after striking naval mines in the south and east of the Strait of Hormuz; there was no independent confirmation or report of a spill, while the IMO has recorded 134 confirmed incidents in the region as of 11 November.

Chain of impact

  • Naval mines and attacks on oil tankers in the strait
  • Fires on board and the risk of cargo spilling
  • Oil slicks carried by the tide into the mangrove channels
  • Grey mangroves' breathing roots smothered
  • Contaminated mud where crabs burrow and feed

Open question

Can a coastal forest remain protected when the sea that feeds it has become a minefield?
